Perplexity tells you the damage. It doesn't tell you where the floor is.

Compressing this model from 8.50 to 2.90 bits per weight costs 11% perplexity and nothing else. The next step down costs 43% perplexity — and half of all long generations collapse into repetition. The quality curve is smooth. The usability curve is a cliff.

What causes it

Weight bit-width — and nothing else we tested. Holding weights fixed and varying KV cache precision across f16, q8_0 and q4_0 produced an identical 4/8 loop rate. KV precision changes which prompts loop, never how often.

The mechanism is attention collapse: heads lock onto a narrow band of recent tokens, the repetition reinforces itself, and the model cannot escape. It happens at full precision too — quantization lowers the threshold for falling in.

What fixes it

Stay at 2.90 bpw or above

For a 27B that is 10 GB and loop-free across every prompt tested.

If you must go lower: the DRY sampler repaired both reproduced loops (diversity 0.18 → 0.57). Note that ordinary repetition penalty causes loops rather than preventing them — it suppresses common necessary words and pushes generation off its natural path.

A quantized V-cache also requires -fa on, or the context will not initialize at all.

Corrections to earlier claims in this investigation

  • Keys tolerate 4-bit but values don't, so the fix is -ctv q8_0. The asymmetry is real for accuracy in the literature, but it does not govern looping: q8 looped exactly as often as q4.
  • Quantized KV cuts output diversity at depth. That matrix used one sample per cell at temperature 0.7, and q4/q4 scored better than q8/q8 — impossible as a real effect, therefore noise.
  • Perplexity does not capture looping. Too strong. Perplexity does rise at the cliff (6.52 → 9.31); what it fails to provide is a threshold. A 43% increase reads as tolerable and is in fact catastrophic.
